## Overview for deal makers

English law is the governing law chosen most often for cross-border commercial contracts, financings and shareholder agreements. England and Wales form a single legal jurisdiction within the United Kingdom. English contract law starts from freedom of contract. The courts of England and Wales read a commercial agreement for what its words mean in context, and they are slow to rewrite a bargain struck between businesses with legal advice. English law has no general duty of good faith in commercial dealings, so a party that wants one must write it in. English courts enforce exclusion and limitation clauses between businesses, subject to the statutory control in the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 on exclusions of negligence liability and on standard terms. They are also subject to the rule that a party cannot exclude liability for its own fraud.

## Key statutes

In business contracts, the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 applies its test of reasonableness to exclusions of liability for negligence (section 2), exclusions and limitations in one party's written standard terms of business (section 3), and exclusions of the terms implied in contracts for the sale and hire of goods (sections 6 and 7). A negotiated exclusion of contractual liability that is not on standard terms falls outside the Act. The Companies Act 2006 governs how companies are formed and managed and how they execute documents, including under section 44. The Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999 lets a third party enforce a term made for its benefit unless the contract excludes the Act. Most commercial contracts do exclude it. The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 adds statutory interest to late payments between businesses, unless the contract gives a substantial remedy of its own. The National Security and Investment Act 2021 requires mandatory notification of acquisitions in seventeen sensitive sectors.

## Courts and regulators

Commercial disputes in England and Wales are heard in the Business and Property Courts of the High Court. These include the Commercial Court and the Technology and Construction Court. Appeals go to the Court of Appeal and then the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom. The Arbitration Act 1996 governs arbitrations seated in England and Wales, and London is a leading seat for international arbitration. The Competition and Markets Authority reviews mergers and enforces competition law, and the Financial Conduct Authority regulates financial services. The Information Commissioner's Office enforces data protection law, and Companies House keeps the public register of companies and their charges.

## Market conventions

English-law commercial contracts usually keep an exclusion of indirect or consequential loss separate from a financial cap, and they list carve-outs from both. A cap is commonly set as a multiple of the charges paid or payable in a twelve-month period. Documents are often executed as deeds where consideration is doubtful or a longer limitation period is wanted. A claim on a deed may be brought within twelve years rather than six. Parties choosing English law often pair it with London arbitration or the exclusive jurisdiction of the English courts.
