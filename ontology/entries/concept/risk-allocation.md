## Definition

Risk allocation is the set of choices in a contract that decide which party bears a loss. The loss may come from a failure to perform, from a third party's claim, or from an event outside either party's control. Without express terms, the general law allocates loss through damages for breach. Rules on remoteness (whether the loss was foreseeable when the contract was made) and mitigation (the duty to limit one's loss) narrow those damages. A commercial contract replaces or adjusts that default with its own terms. Each party can then price the risk it carries and insure against it.

## Why it matters at the table

Most of the time spent negotiating a commercial agreement goes into risk allocation. The price, the scope and the service levels are usually settled before the lawyers start. A party that takes on more risk than it can measure or insure has made a commercial concession, even if the drafting looks routine. The argument that carries most weight is that a risk should sit with the party best able to control it, or to insure it, at the lowest cost. A vendor that charges a modest fee across many customers argues that it cannot price unlimited exposure to each one. A customer whose operations depend on the vendor argues that the vendor controls the cause of any failure.

## How clauses implement it

The limitation of liability clause sets the ceiling on what a party can recover, and it excludes kinds of loss such as indirect and consequential loss. The indemnity clause shifts specified losses, often third-party claims, to one party whoever is at fault, and often outside the cap. Warranties and representations define what counts as a failure, and exclusive remedy provisions limit what a party can claim when one occurs. Insurance clauses make a party carry cover that matches the risk it has accepted. Force majeure clauses release both parties from liability for events outside their control.

## By jurisdiction

### England and Wales

English law gives effect to the risk allocation agreed between businesses, subject to the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977. The Act forbids any exclusion of liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence. It applies a test of reasonableness to other exclusions of negligence liability, to exclusions and limitations in one party's written standard terms of business, and to exclusions of the terms implied in contracts for the sale and hire of goods. A negotiated exclusion of contractual liability that is not on standard terms falls outside the Act. English courts consider whether the parties had equal bargaining power and whether the risk could have been insured.

### India

Indian law gives effect to the risk allocation agreed between commercial parties. Section 73 of the Indian Contract Act 1872 sets the default measure of compensation. Section 74 limits recovery of an agreed sum to reasonable compensation not exceeding the sum named. Indian courts may refuse to enforce a term that is unconscionable or opposed to public policy under section 23.

### Delaware

Delaware courts enforce the risk allocation agreed by sophisticated commercial parties, with few exceptions. The most important is that a party cannot contract out of liability for its own fraud in making the representations in the contract.
