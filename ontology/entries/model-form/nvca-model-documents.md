## What it is

The NVCA model documents are a set of model legal documents for venture capital financings. The National Venture Capital Association, the trade body for the US venture capital industry, publishes them. The NVCA drew up the documents with a working group of venture lawyers. The aim was to cut transaction costs and give both sides a common starting point that favours neither investors nor companies. The NVCA publishes the set free of charge on nvca.org.

## Structure

The NVCA set comprises the term sheet, the stock purchase agreement, the amended and restated certificate of incorporation, the investors' rights agreement, the voting agreement, and the right of first refusal and co-sale agreement. The NVCA also lists ancillary forms among its model documents, such as the management rights letter and the indemnification agreement for directors. Each document offers alternative provisions in brackets. Its footnotes explain the options and the market practice on each point.

## Market acceptance

The NVCA model documents are the accepted starting point for priced equity rounds in US venture capital. Most Series A and later financings for Delaware companies are drafted on them. Investors and companies negotiate by choosing among the bracketed options rather than drafting new provisions. That keeps the negotiation on the economic and control terms.

## Known departures

Departures from the NVCA model commonly concern the liquidation preference (the right to be paid an agreed amount first on a sale or winding up), anti-dilution protection, protective provisions that need investor consent, redemption rights, drag-along terms and information rights. Seed financings often use simpler documents or SAFEs (simple agreements for future equity) instead of the full NVCA set. Some law firms keep their own variants of the NVCA forms.

## Versions

The NVCA revises the model documents from time to time as the law and market practice change. The current versions are the ones posted on nvca.org. Recent revisions have dealt with matters such as US federal rules on outbound investment and tranched financings (money paid in stages). Before relying on a draft's footnotes, check which version the draft was based on.
