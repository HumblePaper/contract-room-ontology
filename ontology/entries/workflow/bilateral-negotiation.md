## What it is

Bilateral negotiation is the workflow in which two parties agree a single contract by exchanging drafts. One party sends a draft, usually on its own form. The other sends back a markup, with its changes shown as tracked changes and its reasons in comments. The parties then keep exchanging revised drafts, issue lists and calls until every open point is settled. Most commercial agreements are agreed this way, including master services agreements and the definitive documents in financings and acquisitions.

## How it runs

Each round has the same shape. The receiving party compares the returned draft with the draft it sent and reviews every change against its playbook. It decides which changes to accept, reject or counter, and records the open points in an issue list. It then sends back its own markup and the issue list, and the parties hold a call to settle what they can. Points the negotiators cannot settle go up to the business owners or the general counsel. The last round usually ends with a clean execution version, which both sides check against the final markup before signing. Some in-house teams now give the first review of each returned draft to an AI agent. A lawyer approves the agent's findings before anything goes back to the counterparty, and the team limits what the agent may read and send.

## What good looks like

A good bilateral negotiation closes in few rounds. Each party answers every point in every round, explains its position, and trades concessions rather than giving them away. Every change in every returned draft is found and reviewed. The issue list matches the draft at every stage, and the signed agreement matches what the parties agreed. For every departure from the playbook, the negotiating counsel can show who approved it and what the party got in return.

## Where it fails

Bilateral negotiation fails when a party relies on the counterparty's tracked changes instead of running its own comparison. Unmarked changes then pass into the signed agreement. It also fails when the issue list and the draft fall out of step, when concessions made on calls go unrecorded, and when the execution version differs from the agreed final draft. Each of these failures produces a signed agreement that differs from the one the party believes it agreed.

## Matter types that use it

The enterprise sale on the customer's paper relies on bilateral negotiation for every deal of any size. Bilateral negotiation is also the workflow for the definitive documents in priced equity rounds and acquisitions, for vendor procurement contracts, for licences and for most amendments.
