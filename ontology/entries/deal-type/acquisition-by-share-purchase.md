## Overview

In an acquisition by share purchase, the buyer takes over the target company by buying its shares. The company passes to the buyer with all its assets, contracts and liabilities. The buyer also takes on the company's history. That is why the warranties, the indemnities and the due diligence carry so much weight. For a private company with a few shareholders, the buyer signs a share purchase agreement with the sellers. For a US public company, the same result usually comes through a statutory merger. A subsidiary of the buyer merges into the target, and the target's shareholders receive cash, buyer shares or both.

## Structure choices

In an acquisition by share purchase, the parties choose between buying shares and buying assets, and between a direct purchase and a merger. They choose between cash and shares as the price, and between a locked-box price (fixed at a past balance sheet date) and completion accounts (adjusted after closing). They also decide whether part of the price is deferred or depends on future results through an earn-out. In US public deals, the parties also choose between a one-step merger that needs a shareholder vote and a two-step tender offer followed by a merger.

## Lifecycle

An acquisition by share purchase starts with an approach and a confidentiality agreement. Due diligence and the negotiation of the definitive agreement follow, then signing. Between signing and closing, the parties satisfy conditions such as shareholder approval and antitrust clearance. Integration work follows closing. Warranty and indemnity claims may be brought within the agreed survival periods.

## Document set

The core document in an acquisition by share purchase is the share purchase agreement or, in a US public deal, the agreement and plan of merger. Around it sit a disclosure schedule, transition services agreements where needed, escrow agreements and financing commitment letters. The parties also make the filings that securities and antitrust regulators require.

## Parties and advisers

The parties to an acquisition by share purchase are the buyer and the sellers or, in a merger, the target company itself. A merger subsidiary of the buyer often joins as well. Each side hires legal counsel and, in larger deals, a financial adviser. Where the price is paid with borrowed money, the buyer's lenders and their counsel join too.

## Economics

The economics of an acquisition by share purchase come down to the price and its form, the price adjustment and any earn-out. The allocation of risk through warranties, indemnities and caps also moves value between the parties. Public deals add termination fees. The target pays one if it accepts a superior offer, and the buyer pays one if regulatory approval fails.

## Where it goes wrong

An acquisition goes wrong when diligence misses a liability that the warranties do not cover, or when the buyer's financing or regulatory approvals fall through. It also goes wrong when a change of control clause in a key customer contract lets the customer end the contract, or when the price adjustment leads to a dispute after closing.

## By jurisdiction

### Delaware

The Delaware General Corporation Law governs mergers of Delaware corporations. Target shareholders who do not vote for a cash merger may have appraisal rights (a right to a court-set price). Merger agreements commonly choose Delaware law and the Court of Chancery.

### England and Wales

In England and Wales, a buyer buys a private company under a share purchase agreement. A buyer takes over a public company by takeover offer or by scheme of arrangement (a court-approved process) under the Takeover Code. The National Security and Investment Act 2021 requires notification in sensitive sectors.

### India

Share acquisitions in India are subject to the Companies Act 2013, the foreign exchange rules for non-resident buyers, merger control by the Competition Commission of India above the notification thresholds, and, for listed targets, the takeover regulations of the Securities and Exchange Board of India.

## By industry

### SaaS

In acquisitions of SaaS companies, buyers focus diligence on recurring revenue and on customer contract terms, including change of control rights and liability caps. They also check data protection compliance and who owns the software and its open source components.
