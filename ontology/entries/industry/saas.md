## Deal landscape

SaaS (software as a service) vendors run a high volume of similar negotiations, and their legal teams judge themselves on speed and on how far the signed terms stray from standard. A SaaS vendor sells access to software that it hosts and runs, usually on an annual or multi-year subscription with fees paid in advance. Small customers accept the vendor's online terms without negotiation. Enterprise customers negotiate, and the largest make the vendor contract on the customer's own paper. SaaS vendors also raise money through priced equity rounds and are frequent targets for acquisition. So their legal teams handle financing and corporate work alongside commercial contracts.

## Documents and forms

A SaaS relationship usually sits in a master subscription or master services agreement, with order forms that set the products, term and fees. A data processing agreement governs how the vendor handles personal data. It often includes standard contractual clauses (approved terms for moving data across borders). A service level agreement sets uptime commitments and the service credits owed when the vendor misses them. Enterprise customers add security schedules, information security questionnaires and audit rights. Many also ask for a SOC 2 report or ISO 27001 certification as evidence of security.

## Clauses that matter

Limitation of liability is the most negotiated clause in a SaaS agreement, because a single data breach can cause losses far larger than the fees the customer pays. SaaS vendors commonly cap liability at the fees paid or payable in the twelve months before the claim. Customers commonly ask for a higher multiple, or for a separate cap for data protection and security breaches. Other clauses that matter are the intellectual property indemnity, the data protection terms, service levels and credits, and suspension rights. So do automatic renewal, price rises on renewal, and what happens to customer data when the contract ends.

## Matter types

The matter type that defines SaaS commercial legal work is the enterprise sale on the customer's paper. In it, the vendor's legal team reviews and negotiates the customer's master agreement against the vendor's playbook. Other recurring matter types are the sale on the vendor's own paper, renewals and amendments, and answers to security questionnaires. The vendor's legal team also handles its own buying of hosting and subprocessor services.
