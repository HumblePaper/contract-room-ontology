## The sequence

After any opening position, the vendor has two steps on the general liability cap. The first step is a cap at the fees paid and payable in the twelve months before the claim. That is the vendor's standard position. The second step, and the vendor's floor, is a cap at the fees paid and payable in the twenty-four months before the claim. Beyond the floor, the vendor does not move the general cap. It negotiates a super-cap (a higher, separate cap) for named categories instead, such as data protection breaches.

## Triggers to move

The vendor moves from twelve to twenty-four months' fees when the customer is an enterprise account above a stated contract value, when the customer's procurement policy sets a minimum cap that the vendor cannot change, or when the customer agrees in return to drop a demand for uncapped liability. The vendor does not move merely because the customer asks a second time. Each move should buy a concession or reflect a documented risk assessment.

## Approval needed at each step

The negotiating counsel may agree the twelve-month cap without further approval, because it is the vendor's standard. Moving to twenty-four months' fees needs the approval of the head of commercial legal or the general counsel, recorded in the deal file. Any cap above twenty-four months' fees, any uncapped liability outside the standard carve-outs, and any super-cap above the vendor's insurance cover need the approval of the general counsel and usually the chief financial officer.
