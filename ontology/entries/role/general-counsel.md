## What the role owns

The general counsel owns the company's legal function and its legal risk. The general counsel sets the company's contracting standards and playbooks, and decides how the legal team is organised and funded. The general counsel also manages outside counsel and answers for the legal side of every significant transaction. In a company that sells to enterprises, the general counsel owns the enterprise sale on customer paper as a matter type. That means the playbook, the approval thresholds and the cycle time that the sales team feels.

## Decisions it takes

The general counsel approves departures from the playbook that go beyond the commercial counsel's authority. Examples are a cap above the vendor's floor, or uncapped liability for one category of claim. The general counsel also decides which matters go to outside counsel, and advises the board on corporate governance and major transactions. When disputes and regulatory inquiries arise, the general counsel decides how the company responds.

## Career path

Most general counsel come from a law firm, often after several years in a transactional or litigation practice. Others rise within an in-house team through roles such as commercial counsel and deputy general counsel. In smaller companies, the first lawyer hired often holds the title of general counsel from the start. The ACC Chief Legal Officers Survey, which ACC runs each year with almost 900 chief legal officers taking part in 2023, describes the role's growing influence in company leadership and its oversight of functions beyond legal.

## Metrics

A general counsel is judged on the legal function's cost against budget, the cycle time of contract negotiations and the share of contracts signed within the playbook. The general counsel is also judged on the outcome of disputes, the absence of avoidable legal losses, and the confidence of the board and the executive team.
