## Citation

Sterling Miller, "Ten Things: Creating a Good Contract Playbook", first published on his Ten Things blog on 17 July 2018. The Association of Corporate Counsel hosts the PDF copy cited here.

## What it establishes

The piece defines a playbook as a document that explains each standard clause, sets out fallback clauses the company would accept, and says when the company will walk away. To build one, the team reviews past contracts for the positions it has agreed, finds where it has walked away, and agrees an escalation process for points beyond the fallbacks. The piece warns against treating a clause accepted once or twice as a position, because the company may have had no choice. It recommends training users every year and reviewing the playbook annually for changes in the contract, the process, customer feedback and the law. It also notes that a playbook is the starting point for AI review of drafts. It is the most practical account on this list of how a playbook is kept up to date.
