## Citation

The Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act No. 9 of 1872), as published on India Code, the official collection of central legislation kept by the Legislative Department of India's Ministry of Law and Justice. The Act came into force on 1 September 1872.

## What it establishes

Section 73 gives a party who suffers from a breach of contract a right to compensation for loss that arose naturally, in the usual course of things, from the breach. The right also covers loss that the parties knew, when they made the contract, was likely to result from the breach. Section 73 also says that no compensation is given for remote and indirect loss. That is the statutory form of the English rule in Hadley v Baxendale. Section 74 deals with a contract that names a sum to be paid on breach, or contains another penalty term. The party complaining of the breach is entitled to reasonable compensation not exceeding the named amount, whether or not it proves actual loss. Section 23 makes an agreement void if its object or consideration is opposed to public policy. Indian courts use section 23 to review the most one-sided exclusion terms.
