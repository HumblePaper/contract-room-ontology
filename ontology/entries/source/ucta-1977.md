## Citation

Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, chapter 50, as published in its revised form on legislation.gov.uk by The National Archives (UK). The revised text shows the amendments made since 1977, including the move of consumer contracts to the Consumer Rights Act 2015.

## What it establishes

The Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (England and Wales) limits how far a business can exclude or cap its liability. Section 2(1) stops a business from excluding or restricting liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence. Section 2(2) lets a business exclude or restrict liability for other loss caused by negligence only if the term is reasonable. Section 3 applies the same test where one party deals on the other's written standard terms of business. It covers any term by which the party relying on it excludes or restricts its liability for breach. Section 11 defines the test: the term must have been fair and reasonable to include, given the circumstances that the parties knew, or ought reasonably to have known, when they made the contract. Where a term caps liability at a specified sum, section 11(4) tells the court to consider the resources available to the party relying on the cap and how far that party could have insured against the liability. Sections 6 and 7 restrict the exclusion of terms implied into contracts for the sale and hire of goods. The Act does not reach every term between businesses. A negotiated exclusion or cap on contractual liability falls outside it if the term is in neither party's written standard terms and does not concern negligence or those implied terms. Schedule 2 lists guidelines on reasonableness, including how strong each party's bargaining position was.
