## Statement

Each party's total aggregate liability arising out of or in connection with the agreement is limited to the total fees paid and payable by the customer under the agreement in the twenty-four months immediately before the event giving rise to the claim. Where the agreement has run for less than twenty-four months, the limit is twice the annual fees. The cap is subject to the agreed carve-outs.

## Rationale

A cap at twenty-four months' fees doubles the customer's protection compared with the twelve-month cap. It still keeps the vendor's exposure tied to the revenue from the contract. The customer can argue that the realistic losses from a serious failure exceed one year's fees. The vendor can accept the higher figure because it stays predictable and insurable.

## When it holds

A cap at twenty-four months' fees holds where the customer is a large enterprise with leverage, the contract is on the customer's paper, the service handles sensitive data or supports a critical process, or the customer's procurement policy sets a minimum cap. It is also used where the vendor wants to keep the general cap tied to fees while refusing uncapped liability for data protection.

## What it costs

For the vendor, a cap at twenty-four months' fees doubles the most it can lose under the contract, and it may need more insurance cover. For the customer, accepting the figure usually means giving up a demand for uncapped liability, or for a fixed cap sized to the possible loss.

## Fallback from and to

A cap at twenty-four months' fees is the vendor's fallback from the twelve-month cap. It is usually the vendor's floor. Beyond it, the vendor's approval matrix sends the decision up to the general counsel or the chief financial officer. A customer reaches the same position by falling back from a demand for a fixed cap or for uncapped liability.
