## Interests

A customer wants to recover its real losses if the vendor fails to perform. That matters most where the vendor holds the customer's data or runs a process the customer's business depends on. A customer also wants control of its data, clear service commitments and protection against price rises. It wants a right to end the contract and get its data back if the service fails.

## Typical positions

A customer usually proposes a cap set at a multiple of the annual fees, or a fixed sum that reflects the possible loss. It asks for higher or unlimited liability for data protection breaches, confidentiality breaches and the vendor's indemnities (promises to cover specified losses). A customer also asks for warranties that the vendor complies with the law and with security standards, for audit rights, and for a narrow definition of the indirect losses that the contract excludes.

## Leverage

A customer has the most leverage before signature, while it can still choose another vendor. It has more still when it is a large enterprise whose name the vendor wants on its client list. A customer's leverage falls once its data and processes sit inside the vendor's service. So customers settle the renewal, exit and data return terms in the first agreement.
