## Interests

A vendor wants its liability under each contract to be predictable and in proportion to the fees it earns. It carries the same exposure across many customers. A vendor also wants payment terms that protect its cash flow, and a contract it can perform with its standard service. It wants to be free to change its product without asking every customer's consent.

## Typical positions

A vendor usually proposes a cap at the fees paid in the previous twelve months, an exclusion of indirect and consequential loss, warranties limited to material conformity with the documentation, and remedies limited to repair, re-performance or service credits (fee discounts for missed service levels). A vendor also tries to keep its intellectual property indemnities narrow. It also seeks to exclude claims that arise from the customer's own changes to the product or from combining it with other products.

## Leverage

A vendor's leverage rises when its product is hard to replace, when switching would cost the customer a lot, and when the customer's project is short of time. A vendor's leverage falls when the customer can run a competitive tender, when the deal is large compared with the vendor's revenue, or when the vendor needs the customer as a reference account.
