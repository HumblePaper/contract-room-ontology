## Profile

Relativity makes e-discovery software (tools to find and review documents for litigation). Law firms, legal departments and government bodies use it to organise and review large volumes of data. It is based in Chicago. Andrew Sieja founded the company, which was earlier called kCura. LawSites wrote in March 2021 that it is widely acknowledged as the market leader for e-discovery software. For contract work, its platform lists contract review among its uses, and its Gavel product drafts documents in Microsoft Word.

## What it sells

Relativity's main platform is Relativity aiR, which succeeded RelativityOne, its software-as-a-service product. Relativity aiR Government holds FedRAMP certification for US government use. Relativity claiR is a conversational AI tool for lawyers. The company lists e-discovery, investigations, data breach response and contract review among the uses of its platform.

## Funding and ownership

Relativity is privately held. In March 2021, LawSites reported that Silver Lake, the private equity firm, had agreed a growth investment that made it Relativity's largest shareholder, with a significant minority stake. LawSites relayed a Wall Street Journal report that the deal valued Relativity at about US$3.6 billion. The other shareholders named were the founder Andrew Sieja and Iconiq Capital, which invested US$125 million in 2015.
