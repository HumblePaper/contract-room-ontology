## Profile

OpenAI builds large language models and sells them through ChatGPT and its API (the interface other software uses to call the models). Its operating company, OpenAI OpCo, LLC, has its registered office in San Francisco. OpenAI was founded in 2015 as a nonprofit. Under a structure announced in October 2025, the nonprofit, now the OpenAI Foundation, controls OpenAI Group PBC, a public benefit corporation (a company that must pursue a stated mission alongside profit). For contract work, the relevant offerings are Astra for Law, its legal configuration of a model, and the ChatGPT plugins that legal vendors build.

## What it sells

OpenAI sells ChatGPT, including business and enterprise plans, and access to its models through its API. In September 2026, Artificial Lawyer reported the launch of Astra for Law, a configuration of the GPT-6 Astra model for legal work. It adds a search index of US case law, statutes and regulations. OpenAI reported that it passed the correctness check on 54.0% of 200 US legal research questions, against 38.7% for GPT-6 Astra using web search alone. Those scores are OpenAI's own. Access started with selected firms.

LawSites reported that the launch came with 26 ChatGPT plugins from legal vendors, of which OpenAI named Harvey, iManage, Legora and Thomson Reuters. Other vendors then announced their own, including an Ironclad plugin that searches a contract repository in plain language. In October 2026, Lets Data Science reported, citing Seeking Alpha, that OpenAI was working with Ironclad to train and test AI agents, with contract lifecycle work as the focus. The terms were not disclosed.

## Funding and ownership

OpenAI's own structure page says that at the October 2025 recapitalisation, the OpenAI Foundation held 26% of OpenAI Group, Microsoft held about 27%, and current and former employees and investors held the remaining 47%. The Foundation appoints the Group's board and can replace its directors at any time.
