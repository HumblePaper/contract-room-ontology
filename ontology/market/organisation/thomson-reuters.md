## Profile

Thomson Reuters sells legal research, practice guidance and software to lawyers, and similar products to tax, accounting, risk and trade professionals. It also owns the Reuters news agency. The company reports its results from Toronto and lists its shares on the Toronto Stock Exchange and Nasdaq under the symbol TRI. For contract work, the products that matter are Practical Law, with its drafting guides and templates, and CoCounsel Legal, its AI assistant for drafting and document review. Westlaw, its research service, supplies the law that CoCounsel Legal draws on.

## What it sells

Westlaw is the company's legal research service. Practical Law holds guides, templates and checklists, which Thomson Reuters says more than 650 attorney-editors write. HighQ is a platform for client collaboration and workflow. CoCounsel Legal is an AI assistant that, the company says, works from Westlaw, Practical Law and a customer's own documents. It drafts from a single request, with linked citations. Its tabular analysis feature runs up to 100 questions across up to 10,000 documents, by the vendor's own account. Reuters reported in August 2026 that Thomson Reuters had launched Thomson 1.0, its own large language model, trained on its legal research content.

In September 2026, LawSites reported that a US federal appeals court, the Third Circuit, ruled for Thomson Reuters against ROSS Intelligence. The court affirmed a lower court's rulings that Westlaw headnotes (editors' summaries of points of law) were original enough for copyright, and that ROSS's use of them to train its system was not fair use. The opinion was still under seal when LawSites reported it, and ROSS said it would appeal to the US Supreme Court.

## Funding and ownership

Thomson Reuters is a listed company. In its results release of 5 February 2026, it reported 2025 revenue of US$7,476 million. Its Legal Professionals segment brought in US$2,868 million of that. The company said Westlaw, CoCounsel and Practical Law drove the segment's organic growth.
