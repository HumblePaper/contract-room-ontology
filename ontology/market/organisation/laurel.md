## Profile

Laurel sells AI that records professionals' working time automatically. It captures time from the tools lawyers and accountants already use and drafts the time entries for billing. LawSites reported in June 2025 that the company was founded in 2016 as Time by Ping, and named Ryan Alshak as its founder and chief executive. It sells to law firms, accounting firms and consulting firms. Its products deal with time and billing, so its place in deal work is the time record behind a law firm's bill.

## What it sells

Laurel's main product is automated timekeeping, with AI-written narratives for each time entry that meet billing rules. It checks entries against client billing rules as they are made. Its site lists a billing product that reviews bills and flags work outside an agreed scope, marked as coming soon in the site menu. It also sells Laurel Signal for project profitability and Laurel Work Studio for dividing work between people and AI. The company claims its users recover up to 30 minutes a day.

## Funding and ownership

Laurel is backed by venture investors. LawSites reported in June 2025 that Laurel raised US$100 million in a Series C led by IVP, with GV (Google Ventures) taking part. The company reported 300% growth year on year and said accounting firms made up half its business. It said its customers included more than 100 of the top legal, accounting and consulting firms.
