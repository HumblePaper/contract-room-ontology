## Summary

LawNext reports that the Third Circuit has published its opinion in Thomson Reuters v. ROSS Intelligence. The court upheld the lower court's ruling for Thomson Reuters, holding that Westlaw headnotes are protected by copyright and that ROSS's use of them to train its AI research tool was not fair use. It called the use minimally transformative, at best, because ROSS built a direct commercial substitute, copying was unneeded, and a market for licensing headnotes as AI training data exists. ROSS says it will seek a full-court rehearing and then Supreme Court review, which matters to anyone licensing data to train legal AI.

## Figures

- 2243, headnotes on which the court affirmed partial summary judgment for Thomson Reuters
- 25000, training memos, approximate number that ROSS had LegalEase Solutions create
