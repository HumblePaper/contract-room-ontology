## Summary

Above the Law columnist Stephen Embry questions surveys that put lawyer AI use at 50 to 70%, because vendors fund many of them and what people say differs from what they do. He draws on a Visier survey of 1,000 US employees, not lawyers, in which 48% said they had exaggerated their AI use or skill. Embry argues that law firms probably behave the same way, with management wanting to believe its AI spending works. He warns that clients will find the gap between claimed and real AI use. Treat adoption figures from vendor-linked surveys with care.

## Figures

- 48%, US employees who said they exaggerated their AI usage or expertise (Visier survey of 1,000)
- 45%, respondents who felt pressured to use AI despite lacking confidence
- 33%, respondents who believe leaders understand training needs
- 28%, respondents who believe leaders understand current AI usage
- 15%, respondents who say workplaces are keeping up with AI skill alignment, against 56% who call it critical
