## The short answer

Yes. A cap at the fees paid and payable in the twelve months before the claim is the most common general cap in SaaS contracts, and most SaaS vendors' standard terms use it. A cap at twelve months' fees is market as a starting point. It holds in most mid-market deals on vendor paper.

## When customers obtain more

Enterprise customers often get a cap at twenty-four months' fees or a multiple of the annual fees. That is most likely when they contract on their own paper or trust the vendor with sensitive data. Customers also commonly get a separate, higher super-cap (a higher cap for named claims) for data protection and security breaches. The intellectual property indemnity is often left outside the cap altogether.

## What the answer rests on

The answer that a twelve-month cap is market rests on practitioner consensus, not on a published survey. The position varies with the size of the deal, the sensitivity of the data and the bargaining power of the parties. In England and Wales, a cap in written standard terms must also pass the test of reasonableness under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977. That test takes account of the insurance available to the vendor.
