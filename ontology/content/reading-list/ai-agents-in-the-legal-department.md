## What this list covers

AI agents are software that take a legal task as a goal and carry out its steps. Typical tasks are sorting requests, checking a contract against the playbook (the team's written negotiating positions) and proposing a first markup. In 2026 in-house legal departments started putting agents to work on contracts. This list covers the departments building their own agents, what vendors sell agents to do, and how well legal AI performs when measured against lawyers. It ends with the controls lawyers keep, the professional duties that apply, and the pressure on departments to adopt. Most pieces are from 2026. Two are vendor marketing and one is a vendor survey, and each note says so.

## Departments building their own

AT&T's legal department is the largest in-house legal department to announce such a partnership publicly, as far as this list found. Artificial Lawyer reports its collaboration with OpenAI on top of LegalEdge, the in-house team AT&T started in January 2026. Lawyer Monthly reports the same move as a plan to rely less on outside law firms. Legal IT Insider's report on the FT Innovative Lawyers Europe 2026 awards describes general counsel in Europe building agents too.

## What agents are sold to do

Artificial Lawyer's report of Checkbox's First Pass describes an agent placed at intake, checking each contract against the playbook before a lawyer reads it. Harvey's post on its review agents is vendor marketing. It is on the list because it describes the gap between a written playbook and what a team will actually accept.

## How well they work

The Vals Legal AI Report of February 2025 was run by a company independent of the vendors it tested, though Vals discloses a customer relationship with one or more participants. The report was 20 months old when this list was published. It found the best tool well ahead of lawyers at answering questions about a document. It found lawyers ahead at redlining, which in this list's view is the task closest to reviewing a markup.

## Controls and professional duties

The Law.com piece explains how in-house teams scope an agent's instructions, access and permissions. The Bar Council's document sets out, for England and Wales, the barrister's continuing responsibility for AI output and the duty to protect confidential material. Reed Smith's guide covers the contract a department signs when it buys an agent.

## Pressure to adopt

The DISCO survey, reported by Canadian Lawyer, found boards and executives pressing in-house teams to adopt AI. DISCO's James Park said that "pricing transparency has become a bigger constraint on adoption than output".

## What to read first

Start with the Vals Legal AI Report, because it is free to read, comes from a company independent of the vendors it tested, and measures legal AI against lawyers on contract tasks. Then read the Bar Council's document, which states plainly who answers for AI output in England and Wales. Then read the Law.com piece on how in-house teams limit what agents may do, after a free registration.
